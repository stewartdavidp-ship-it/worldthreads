# A year grows into a historical network

WorldThreads begins with a focused year. Build a source-grounded foundation, expose its gaps, invite local research, verify contributions and grow the graph. Open another year when the foundation and review process are working well enough to support further work. Continue accepting research for earlier years; a new year does not close them.

## The cycle

1. **Seed a year:** establish bounded observations across relevant places and systems, source passages, explicit relationships, competing explanations and research gaps. Do not equate archival density with historical importance.
2. **Review the foundation:** inspect key claims independently, fix provenance and chronology, and distinguish measurements, accounts and inference. A clean structural audit is necessary but does not replace historical review.
3. **Invite local exploration:** readers reach the frontier of a thread, choose to continue or start another perspective, and investigate that year in a locality they care about. Suggested missions disclose provisional theories, missing evidence and what would support or challenge them.
4. **Verify and incorporate:** preserve drafts, review sources and alternatives, assign stable IDs, document corrections and accept supported records through repository review. Award recognition for verified discoveries, challenges, resilience and source work.
5. **Open the next year:** create a seed and review queue for a deliberately selected year. The sequence may follow an outcome backward or forward, rather than mechanically choosing the next calendar year.
6. **Connect years:** after relevant yearly records exist, review proposed cross-year connections as their own claims. Keep their lag, mechanism, alternatives, confidence and evidence separate from the annual observations.

## Readiness to expand

Readiness is a recorded editorial decision, not an automatic score. Before opening another year, document:

- A reviewed seed covering the main intended systems and multiple regions, with remaining omissions explicit.
- Independent review of the key observations and causal claims that organize the seed.
- Working source provenance, correction history, IDs, audit and contribution intake.
- A tested contribution-to-review-to-acceptance path and a realistic review capacity.
- Research missions that welcome competing explanations and inconclusive results.
- A reason for selecting the next year, its initial scope, seed sources and reviewer responsibilities.

1816 is the active prototype. Its independent review is pending and public hosted submission is not yet implemented, so this project does not currently claim expansion readiness. No subsequent year is selected or opened by this document.

## Across-year causality

Annual views are entry points into one graph, not separate truths. IDs remain globally unique and accepted records retain their event dates and source identity. Later annual views reference the same record rather than duplicate it. Maintain a central record registry before loading several yearly datasets together; existing year-prefixed IDs already provide a namespace.

A proposed cross-year relationship records subject and object IDs, originating and outcome dates, lag, mechanism, causal status, evidence, alternatives and reviewer decision. For example, an 1816 condition followed by an 1817 response can be compared across places once those yearly records are reviewed. Temporal sequence alone still cannot establish the mechanism.

Look for persistent conditions, delayed outcomes, feedback, adaptation and reversals. Compare similar shocks with different outcomes and similar outcomes with different antecedents. Check institutional changes, trade, population, land use and reporting alongside environmental explanations. More years enlarge the comparison set; they do not automatically prove causality.

Keep annual research contributions anchored to their target year. Antecedents and outcomes in other years can be cited as context until their own records are seeded and reviewed. Proposed cross-year links enter a distinct review queue; they never bypass review because both endpoints are already accepted.
