# WorldThreads: uncoached single-player test

Use a fresh browser profile, then repeat later with that same profile. Do not erase a player's existing work. This protocol covers the human checks automation cannot establish.

## First impression — 3 minutes

Show the story shelf. Say only: “Choose something you want to understand.” Observe where the player begins. Ask: “What are you trying to find out? What would count as success?” Record their own words; do not explain the badges first.

## Investigation — 15 minutes

Say: “Follow your question and build an argument you could defend. Find something that supports it and something that could change your mind.” Record wrong turns, unexplained terms, repeated clicks, skipped evidence, help requested and whether the player reads source boundaries. Let a dead end remain a dead end.

If the player cannot proceed, record the exact point and time before offering help. Distinguish interface help from historical reasoning help. Do not count a coached completion as an uncoached success.

## Research return — 10 minutes

Ask the player to choose a missing link, prepare an AI research brief and bring back one candidate. They should inspect its principal source, date/place scope and passage before assigning an evidence role. Observe whether format acceptance is mistaken for verification. If the source is inaccessible, an honest unresolved research lead is a successful outcome.

## Thesis and rewards — 10 minutes

Ask: “What is your claim now? What changed it? What still needs research?” Have them attempt the portfolio preparation checklist. Record whether its requirements explain the next action. Ask: “What do your points or badges prove?” The desired distinction is completed research practice versus verified historical truth.

## Return after several days — 5 minutes

Open the preserved profile. Ask the player to resume without instructions, explain the argument and identify the next source to seek. Record time to recover purpose and whether saved evidence, limits and revision history help.

## Record one row per observed issue

Case ID from the test plan; starting screen; action; expected outcome; actual outcome; player's explanation; assistance given; severity; screenshot with consent; proposed fix; repeat-test result.

Evaluate comprehension separately from completion. A player who collects every badge but cannot explain why a source changes a claim has completed the mechanics, not demonstrated the intended learning. A defensible bounded argument with an acknowledged gap may be a stronger outcome than an overconfident complete portfolio.
