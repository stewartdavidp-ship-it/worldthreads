# WorldThreads: a short uncoached review

Open https://stewartdavidp-ship-it.github.io/worldthreads/ in a fresh browser profile. Spend about 15–20 minutes. Please say what you are thinking as you go; there are no grades or expected thesis answers. The observer should record where you hesitate and avoid explaining the controls unless you are stuck.

1. Before clicking, describe what you think the site is for and what you expect to accomplish.
2. Choose the Europe–Korea story. Follow it until you can state your own provisional explanation. When something is unclear, show where you would look for help.
3. Show which details came from records, which are your interpretation, and which question remains unanswered. Explain why you draw those boundaries.
4. Find a useful direction for investigating that unanswered question. Inspect one record, return, and write the next evidence you would seek in your own words.
5. At the ending, show what you would do next and why. Do the optional choices feel available without distracting you from that next step?
6. Open the field journal. Choose one badge, explain what it recognises and how to earn it. Does an earned badge mean the historical conclusion is correct?

If you already have a filed thesis and an AI critique, try returning that critique to WorldThreads. Describe what the site lets you do that you would miss by staying in the assistant. If you do not have those materials, skip this task rather than inventing a review.

For the observer: record the participant's words, detours, requests for help and any lost work. Mark each task as completed independently, completed with help, or unfinished. Pay special attention to whether they infer a cause from contrasting reports, mistake a lead for evidence, or mistake a badge for scholarly certification. These are usability observations; do not treat completion time as a grade.
