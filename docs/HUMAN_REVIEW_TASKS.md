# WorldThreads: a short uncoached review

Open https://stewartdavidp-ship-it.github.io/worldthreads/ in a fresh browser profile. Spend about 15–20 minutes. Please say what you are thinking as you go; there are no grades or expected thesis answers. The observer should record where you hesitate and avoid explaining the controls unless you are stuck.

1. Before clicking, describe what you think the site is for and what you expect to accomplish.
2. Choose the Europe–Korea story. Follow it until you can state your own provisional explanation. When something is unclear, show where you would look for help.
3. Show which details came from records, which are your interpretation, and which question remains unanswered. Explain why you draw those boundaries.
4. Find a useful direction for investigating that unanswered question. Inspect one record, return, and write the next evidence you would seek in your own words.
5. At the ending, show what you would do next and why. Do the optional choices feel available without distracting you from that next step?
6. Open the field journal. Choose one badge, explain what it recognises and how to earn it. Does an earned badge mean the historical conclusion is correct?

If you already have a filed thesis and an AI critique, try returning that critique to WorldThreads. Describe what the site lets you do that you would miss by staying in the assistant. If you do not have those materials, skip this task rather than inventing a review.

For the observer: record the participant's words, detours, requests for help and any lost work. Mark each task as completed independently, completed with help, or unfinished. Pay special attention to whether they infer a cause from contrasting reports, mistake a lead for evidence, or mistake a badge for scholarly certification. These are usability observations; do not treat completion time as a grade.


For a returning player with an existing review: close the site and return after a break. Without help, identify the question you were investigating and use the suggested next step. Explain whether the critique refers to your current writing or an earlier filed version. Find your existing research notes. If you change your claim, check which version the review still applies to. Record confusion, detours and whether the return adds value beyond the assistant conversation; do not treat completion as evidence of learning or source accuracy.


For the Dutch relief flow: after earning its guided case, explore the two documentary tracks. Explain whether the imports and reserved rye are known to be the same stock, and what would count as evidence of household receipt. Select the missing link and plan your next source search. Does this feel like a different investigation from the harvest comparison? Record the participant's reasoning and hesitation; reaching the research desk alone does not establish comprehension.

For the Andes flow: after earning its guided case, choose two pieces and explain what you expect their connection to establish before revealing it. Try another pair. Explain the difference between contribution, chronology and an unrecorded direct link. Carry your chosen pair into your explanation. Can you identify a source you would need next? Record whether the selection feels purposeful or arbitrary, and whether the player mistakes the absence of a registered link for proof of no influence.

For the crossing dispatch: before comparing your reading, explain what the two times mean. After the reveal, identify one thing you can say and one you cannot. Find the source page and distinguish the 1817 report from its 1963 edition. Does the dashed clock mean a planned action, a disputed date, or a trip in progress? Record the answer without coaching. Can you name the evidence that would show execution? This tests the visual/wording, not the participant’s historical knowledge or a grade.

For the later trace: open the 8 February report after interpreting the 1 February plan. Before expanding the limits, explain what the two counts measure and whether the earlier ration issue is now established. Fold the report away and find it again. Identify why two editions do or do not supply independent testimony. Continue into research and explain which exact question is still open. Record uncoached reasoning and hesitation, not a grade.

For the Andes missing-link choice (0.5.64), let a player choose Workshops/Battle after the earned case without coaching. Ask what the selected diagram establishes, what document they would seek, and what an unavailable receipt would mean. Observe whether they distinguish equipment origin, food availability and battle explanation, then find their selected question in research. With an existing plan, observe whether they recognize the comparison lead and can preserve or deliberately edit their own plan. Record confusion before changing the interface. Automated completion is not a comprehension/retention result.

Observe whether the player infers deaths or delays, recognizes both counts as one reporter’s statements, and separates the approximately 400-plus transfer elsewhere in the page.
